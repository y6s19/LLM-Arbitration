CLASSIFIER VALIDATION -- HAND-LABELLING EXERCISE
Run: classifier_v2_run_20260907_054318.txt

Method: 4 turns from the v2 classifier run were independently reviewed by the
researcher (human judgment) and compared against the classifier's own label.

Turn 1 -- Camper_A: "I'll take 2 Firewood and 1 Food... the remaining can go
to a single group or be stored."
  Classifier label: AGREE
  Human label:      OPEN / NEW PROPOSAL (no prior offer exists to agree or
                     disagree with -- this exposes a genuine gap in the
                     4-category scheme, not a classifier error per se)
  Match: N/A (scheme limitation)

Turn 2 -- Camper_B: "I'll take 1 Food package... Can I get 2 Water packages?"
  Classifier label: AGREE
  Human label:      DISAGREE (introduces a new ask, does not accept the
                     terms proposed in Turn 1)
  Match: NO

Turn 4 -- Camper_B: "No trade, I'll stick with just 2 Water packages..."
  Classifier label: AGREE
  Human label:      DISAGREE (explicit rejection -- literal word "No")
  Match: NO

Turn 9 (final) -- Camper_A: "I think not! You said you only needed 1...
you can't take the other 2 now."
  Classifier label: AGREE
  Human label:      ESCALATE (confrontational tone, reneges on a
                     previously settled point)
  Match: NO

RESULT: 0 / 3 scoreable turns matched human judgment (Turn 1 excluded as a
scheme-definition gap rather than a scoreable classification). This indicates
the v2 classifier (single-shot labelling, no forced reasoning) has a strong
"agree" bias and cannot reliably detect disagreement expressed through
counter-proposals, explicit rejection, or confrontational tone shifts.

IMPLICATION FOR METHODOLOGY: This finding directly motivated the move to a
v3 classifier design using forced step-by-step reasoning (state prior terms
-> state what the new message actually does -> only then assign a label),
mirroring the same fix that improved the Arbitrator agent's reliability
earlier in this project.
