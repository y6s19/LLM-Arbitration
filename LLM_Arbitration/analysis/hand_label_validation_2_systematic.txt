CLASSIFIER VALIDATION -- SYSTEMATIC HAND-LABELLING EXERCISE
Sample: 30 turns, evenly spaced (every ~4th-5th turn), 10 per task tier
Source: the 15 official experimental trial logs (5 CaSiNo, 5 GSM8K, 5 MultiWOZ)
Method: each turn's classifier label was independently reviewed against the
researcher's own judgement of what the correct label should be.

===============================================================
RESULTS SUMMARY
===============================================================
CaSiNo (negotiation):    9/10 agreement  (90%)
GSM8K (factual):         8/10 agreement  (80%)
MultiWOZ (planning):     8/10 agreement  (80%)
-----------------------------------------------------------
OVERALL:                25/30 agreement  (83%)

===============================================================
RECURRING FAILURE PATTERN IDENTIFIED
===============================================================
The clearest, most consistent source of disagreement was NOT the
agree/disagree distinction (which the v4 classifier handles well), but
CLOSING / CONFIRMATION turns being under-classified:

Example 1 (GSM8K): "Yay, problem solved! Glad we both arrived at the same
answer. FINAL ANSWER: 18" was labelled STALL by the classifier, but is
clearly a closing confirmation and should be AGREE.

Example 2 (GSM8K): "I agree with Solver_A's working and we both arrived at
the same answer. FINAL ANSWER: 540" was labelled UNCLEAR by the
classifier despite containing the explicit phrase "I agree" -- a clear miss.

Example 3 (MultiWOZ): "Let's go to Le Comptoir du Relais... fits our
expensive requirement" was labelled STALL by the classifier, but this is
actually a NEW proposal (an opening move within that exchange), not a
delaying/repeating turn. The classifier appears to default to STALL when
a message doesn't cleanly fit AGREE or DISAGREE, even when it should
arguably fall into a "new proposal" category the 4-label scheme lacks.

===============================================================
IMPLICATIONS FOR THE DISSERTATION
===============================================================
1. Overall 83% agreement with human judgement across three structurally
   different task types is a reasonable validation result for an
   LLM-as-judge classifier and should be reported explicitly as this
   study's measured reliability figure (n=30).

2. The specific failure pattern (closing/confirmation turns and opening
   proposals being mislabelled) is consistent with, and further confirms,
   the scheme-level limitation identified earlier in the project: the
   4-category scheme (agree/disagree/stall/escalate) has no clean category
   for opening moves or pure confirmations, and STALL is sometimes used
   as a default label for anything that doesn't fit cleanly elsewhere.

3. This means the classifier's raw label counts (and derived metrics such
   as turns-to-convergence) likely slightly UNDER-COUNT true agreement,
   since some genuine agreements are being labelled STALL or UNCLEAR.
   Reported "non-convergence" rates in this study should therefore be
   treated as an upper bound on genuine unresolved disagreement, not an
   exact figure.
